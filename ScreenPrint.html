What was this project about

The aim of the shared canvas project was to make an impact through art. In this case, the form of art was screen printing. The goal of my piece was to show people how our democracy is falling apart and how many people are simply standing on the sidelines instead of trying to make a difference. In addition to creating a screen print, we also made a documentary that followed the process of creating the frame and final product using b-roll and skills we had learned in Adobe Premiere Pro.

How We Made our Screen Prints

Making the screen-printing frames was a long process that we completed in class over the course of a few weeks. To start the project, we had to figure out what we were going to feature in the artwork and what our intended impact would be. I came up with a sketch of a man standing in front of a building labeled “Democracy” that was slowly breaking down and falling apart. Once I refined the sketch and solidified my idea, we brought the project into Adobe Photoshop and added myself into the design, along with a more detailed drawing of the building. After making those elements look more polished and professional, I began the physical process of building the frame. I started by cutting and measuring wooden planks to fit the dimensions needed for the size of my print. I got those measurements from the dimension and color guide we had created earlier in the process. After cutting the wooden pieces, we securely glued and stapled them together. In the next class, once the glue was dry, I stapled on the mesh that would hold the vinyl with my artwork cut out of it. Once we cut out the vinyl and ironed it onto the center of the screen-printing frame, it was time to print. I printed on three different materials: paper, fabric, and a cardboard box. We then finished the process by creating a documentary that followed each step.
