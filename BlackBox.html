Black box investigation

Intro

In our New Media and Tech class we were challenged with coming up with and building what we thought would be in the black box. The Mystery black box is what it sounds like, a black box that, when you pour different amounts of water into it, different amounts of water would come out.

CER

Based on my group's research we can conclude that there is not a tipping bucket inside the black box. When building our model, we started with creating sketches of what we thought would be inside, then combined and chose the ideas we thought would work best. The idea we landed on was a tipping bucket that would tip over when it was filled with enough water. To start building, we wrote a design proposal with a material list that would outline what we were going to build. We had 2 days to build the box. On day one, we started by cutting off the flaps so we could see inside. Then we added the tip bucket with a chopstick as the axis. As well as adding a funnel on the top of the box. On day 2, we were able to make the tipping bucket more functional as well as testing it a few times. To test our model we used a graduated cylinder and filled it to the 100 mL mark. We started pouring that amount of water into the funnel until the bucket would eventually tip over. Although the bucket was efficient at tipping over, it only did so when it was completely full. That means that there was only one amount of water ever leaving the bucket. Contradicting the way that multiple different amounts of water that would exit The Mystery Black Box. The Models were useful because it was able to allow us to test and explore what was inside of the Mystery Black Box. However there were limitations on materials. The real Mystery Black Box is a big plastic box and most likely accompanied by quality materials on the inside. We understandably just weren't given the same materials. On the other hand, one of the general strengths is the freedom and ability to change ideas and problem solve as the project resumes. If the data had not supported our claim, we would have seen that there was a different amount of water exiting the box after every test. Instead, the data showed that our claim was correct in that there is not a tipping bucket inside the mystery back box.

3D design

Maya Autodesk

We modeled our version of the Black Box using a 3D rendering program called Maya. This process was similar to the one of 2D modeling. We started by just making a basic model with most of the basic inner parts. Over the course of a few weeks we were able to learn all of the different ways we could make parts like tubes and putting holes in objects. Again we did this through different pictionary prompts at the beginning of class. After getting comfortable with the basics and different parts of the program we learned how to animate the different parts of the box. We animated the buckets inside tipping over, the flaps moving, etc. Some students including myself were able to animate water moving through the box.

Photography

In our project we used photography to capture what we were building and how. We also used it to capture and practice our use of photography. For example seeing the role of thirds, framing, and angle in our photos. More specifically, we took photos of group members working on the box, 2D and 3D modeling.

2D Design

Adobe Photoshop

We used 2D design to create a sketch of what was inside the box we built. After learning how to operate we did so, but once we got more comfortable with it we learned how to animate our drawing move. For example the buckets inside and/or water moving through the 2D sketch. One of the ways we were able to get used to it was through Pictionary prompts to start off every class. That's also when we were able to learn skills like animating the drawings.
